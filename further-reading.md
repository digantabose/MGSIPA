# Further Reading and Resource Guide

This document is intended as a companion to the MGSIPA training session:

**The Need for Caution While Using AI in Education: Challenges, Ethics,
Integrity and Governance Issues**

The resources are grouped by purpose rather than by publication date.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. Academic integrity and assessment

### UGC, Promotion and Maintenance of Standards of Academic Integrity and Prevention of Plagiarism in Higher Educational Institutions Regulations

**Year:** 2018

**Read this for:** intellectual honesty, plagiarism, academic integrity
and institutional responsibilities in Indian higher education.

https://www.ugc.gov.in/regulations/UGC_Regulations_university

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### Walters, W. H., & Wilder, E. I. (2023)

**Fabrication and errors in the bibliographic citations generated by
ChatGPT.**

*Scientific Reports, 13*, 14045.

https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-41032-5

**Why it matters:** Provides empirical evidence that AI-generated
references can be fabricated or contain substantive bibliographic
errors.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### Liang, W., et al. (2023)

**GPT detectors are biased against non-native English writers.**

*Patterns, 4*(7), 100779.

https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2023.100779

**Why it matters:** Useful when discussing fairness, AI detectors and
high-stakes academic decisions involving multilingual and non-native
English writers.

## 6. AI adoption and student perspectives

### Digital Education Council, Global AI Student Survey 2024

https://www.digitaleducationcouncil.com/newsroom/what-students-want-key-results-from-dec-global-ai-student-survey-2024

**Useful findings:** AI adoption, weekly use, student AI readiness,

## 10. Search strategy for keeping this repository current

AI policy changes quickly. Do not rely on a static list of articles.

For future updates, periodically check:

-   Ministry of Education
-   UGC
-   CBSE
-   MeitY
-   UNESCO
-   OECD
-   NIST
-   official university policy pages
-   official AI vendor documentation

For legal questions, always prioritise the current primary source over a
blog or social-media summary.

For research questions, verify claims against the original paper, DOI
record or publisher page.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 11. Questions to ask when evaluating a new source

Before adding a paper or policy document to an institutional AI resource
list, ask:

1.  Who published it?
2.  When was it published?
3.  What population does it study?
4.  What methodology was used?
5.  Is it empirical research, policy guidance, commentary or marketing?
6.  Are the findings generalisable?
7.  Are the AI systems tested still representative of current systems?
8.  Are the legal provisions still current?
9.  Does the source distinguish evidence from recommendation?
10. Can the original data or document be accessed?

This prevents old or highly specific findings from being presented as
universal current facts.

## 13. Final principle

The objective of AI governance in education should be neither
unrestricted adoption nor blanket prohibition.

The objective is to establish conditions under which AI can provide
educational value while preserving:

-   learning
-   human agency
-   privacy
-   fairness
-   academic integrity
-   transparency
-   accountability
-   institutional trust
